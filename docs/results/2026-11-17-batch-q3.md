## Promotion loses each artifact's preamble

`FoundationFile._extract_entries` slices an artifact from each declaration to the next and stores
each slice as an entry. Whatever sits above the first declaration belongs to no entry, so `save`
never writes it into `LMS/Foundation.lean`. #105 kept the imports, but the other lines up there were
still lost. For the 27 artifacts verified in this batch:

| Lines above the first declaration | Track B (25 verified) | Track A (2 verified) |
|---|--:|--:|
| `open CategoryTheory` (several also `open scoped CategoryTheory`) | 25 | 0 |
| `variable` binders, such as `variable (p : S ⥤ C)` | 12 | 0 |
| Kept in the foundation at the end | 0 | 2 |

Every failed build fits this, and #105's error logging now shows Lean's own messages:

- `Unknown identifier S`, `Unknown identifier p.obj`: the `variable` binders are gone.
- `Unknown identifier Category`: the `open` is gone.
- `expected token` at `⥤`, `⟶` and `≫`: this notation is scoped to the `CategoryTheory`
  namespace, so without the `open` it does not parse.
- `unexpected identifier; expected 'lemma'`: a structure field's docstring left at top level
  after that parse error.

A replay of the merge at the pin, on the run's first verified artifact, writes
`def IsCartesian {X Y : S}` with `S` at line 36, column 25. The run's first build error is
`Foundation.lean:36:25: Unknown identifier S`.

Two consequences, as in q2:

- **Duplicated work.** A rollback reopens the tag, and the chair assigns it again. The 8M run
  verified 02XK (*cartesian morphism*) **9 times** and 003Y twice, and ended with an empty
  foundation. 9 of its 11 verified artifacts duplicate an earlier one.
- **R9 is still C9.** Both kept nothing. R9's reset ran every generation and dropped 0 entries
  each time.

The fix is #107: each artifact's lines above its first declaration are written with its entries
in a `section` of their own. Replayed in run order under #107, the 8M run's 11 verified artifacts
give 4 foundation entries (`IsCartesian`, `IsCartesianOver`, `IsStronglyCartesian`,
`CategoryOver`). The other 7 repeat one of those names and would be refused, if the merged module
builds against Mathlib. Only the server can check that.

## The thinking sweep

Four arms on track B with one configuration: caps of 8K, 32K (two seeds; the 8M run's first 3M
tokens are the second) and 64K tokens of thinking per reply. Thinking was 79–82% of reply
characters in every run, as in q2. Thinking tokens below are estimated from characters, at about
3.2 characters per token.

| Cap | Replies | Median thinking per reply | 99th percentile | Replies at ≥90% of the cap | Verified by 3M tokens |
|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| 8K | 363 | ~4.0K | ~8.4K | 10.7% | 3 |
| 32K (think32b) | 318 | ~3.8K | ~13.6K | 0% | 5 |
| 32K (8M run) | 903 | ~3.7K | ~14.1K | 0.2% | 3 |
| 64K | 332 | ~3.8K | ~13.2K | 0% | 4 |

The model stops thinking on its own at about 4K tokens per reply. A cap can only cut thinking
short, so the 32K and 64K arms got the same treatment as each other, and only the 8K cap ever
took effect, on about one reply in ten. The two 32K seeds differ by as much (3 against 5) as any
two arms. This sweep says nothing about whether more thinking helps. Testing that needs caps
below the median (1K, 2K, 4K against uncapped) or a prompt that asks for more.

## Track A

- **The import fix works here.** Two verified artifacts reached the foundation as 4 entries and
  stayed: 012U (`SpectralSequence`, `Filtration`, `ConvergesTo`) and 011Q (`ExactCouple`).
- **012U is vacuous again**, in the second batch running. `ConvergesTo` has three free `Prop`
  fields (`bounded`, `regular`, `complete`), and its abutment lands in a `gr` that is a free
  type, not the quotient of one filtration step by the next. `SpectralSequence` has only an `E_infty` page. It verified, passed every gate and
  closed the tag.
- **It became the most-cited entry.** 21 of 48 artifacts cited earlier work (22 citations of an
  earlier generation): 14 cite 012U, 8 cite `ExactCouple`. All 21 failed: 6 on parse errors, 6 on
  imports of modules that don't exist, 4 on unknown names, the rest on other errors. Agents now try to build on the foundation, and the first thing they
  build on is a structure that states nothing.
- `ExactCouple` is a real definition of an exact couple, over additive groups rather than an
  abelian category; its three exactness conditions match the Stacks statement.

## GPUs: one per job, four lanes

**TP=1 serves.** All 7 LMS jobs and the TP=1 speed job served at base settings on the first
try, in 50–171 s; the MTP job stopped on a setting (below). With one GPU
per job there is no NCCL, so the unbridged-pair hang of q1 and q2 cannot occur.

**Four lanes fit.** All four first jobs started within a minute of each other, at 96 GB of host
memory each. The check that lanes 2–4 share lane 1's built library without rebuilding it passed
in all 6 of their jobs.

**Speed per run.** The batch was sized at 60% of TP=2's speed. It got about 80%:

| | q2, TP=2 | q3, TP=1 | Ratio |
|--|--:|--:|--:|
| Track B, output tokens/s | 135 (8.17M in 10.3 h) | 108 (8.01M in 13.1 h) | 80% |
| Track A, output tokens/s | 146 (4.05M in 4.4 h) | 111 (4.29M in 6.2 h) | 76% |

Lane 1 took 13.1 h against an estimate of 17 h. The LMS jobs used 40.2 GPU-hours.

**Where GPU time went.** From the first start to the last end the batch held a 52-GPU-hour
window. About 11–12 GPU-hours of it went unused by the batch, all at the end, while lane 1 ran
alone. The larger loss is inside the running jobs:

- **Running jobs used little of their GPUs.** No run had more than 3 requests in flight, and none used more than
  10.6% of its KV cache.
- **What one TP=1 GPU can serve** (thr3, base settings):

| Requests at once | Output tokens/s | Per request | Prompt-heavy pass: output tokens/s | Per request |
|----:|----:|----:|----:|----:|
| 1 | 79 | 79 | | |
| 3 | 175 | 58 | 164 | 55 |
| 9 | 497 | 55 | 436 | 48 |
| 18 | 922 | 51 | 755 | 42 |
| 36 | 1,626 | 45 | | |
| 72 | 2,588 | 36 | | |

A run uses about 3 requests' worth of what its GPU serves. Three runs sharing one GPU (9 requests)
would each go 5–13% slower, by these figures. That would triple the runs per GPU.

**MTP did not start.** With `qwen3_next_mtp` and 2 speculative tokens, vLLM stopped at start-up:
`max_num_seqs (1024) exceeds available Mamba cache blocks (987)`. Starting with `--max-num-seqs`
at or below 987 should get past it. MTP lowers the time per token of a single stream, which is
what an LMS run is limited by.
